      Agrarian Reform Law

      Fidel signing the Agrarian Reform Law.
      Text typeLaw
      PromulgationMay 17 , 1959
      RepealOctober 3 , 1963
      Country Cuba

      The Agrarian Reform Law , or First Agrarian Reform Law (a second one was passed in 1963), was a law of the Cuban revolutionary government that abolished large landholdings and sharecropping . Its fundamental objective was the redistribution of the country's land, favoring the poorest peasants. The law harmed the interests of both domestic and foreign landowners and turned them against the Cuban Revolution.

      It was the most significant measure adopted by the Revolution in its first phase, benefiting over 100,000 peasant families and dealing a severe blow to large landholdings and imperialist domination of Cuba. It abolished the right of foreign companies and individuals to own land in Cuba, except for small farmers.

      It set the maximum amount of land a person could own at 30 caballerías (402 hectares). It established the National Institute of Agrarian Reform (INRA) to implement the adopted measures, which would be headed by Prime Minister Fidel Castro . The Law consisted of 15 clauses, 67 articles, 7 transitional provisions, and one final provision. The latter conferred constitutional status upon the Agrarian Reform Law by declaring it part of the Fundamental Law of the Republic [1] .

      Causes

      Among the reasons given by the Cuban revolutionary government for the enactment of the First Agrarian Reform Law were:

      • Land ownership was concentrated in the hands of a few, with 2,336 estates controlling an area of ​​317,000 caballerías (approximately 1,300 hectares). This meant that 1.5% of landowners possessed more than 46% of the national land area.
      • The underutilization of land on large farms, where cultivated areas were maintained in low-yield production, excessive areas were used in extensive livestock farming , and other areas that needed to be rescued for productive activities were still completely idle and sometimes covered with marabou .
      • The need for growth and diversification of Cuban industry, to facilitate the more efficient use of its natural and human resources and the elimination of dependence on agricultural monoculture.
      • To facilitate the emergence and expansion of new crops that would provide the national industry with raw materials and satisfy the needs of food consumption, consolidating and expanding the lines of agricultural production destined for export, a source of foreign exchange for the necessary imports.
      • To increase the population's purchasing power by progressively raising the standard of living of rural inhabitants, thereby contributing to the expansion of the domestic market.
      • To lift the vast majority of Cuba's rural population out of their traditional state of misery.
      • Eliminate sharecropping .
      • Eliminate large-scale, extensive and uneconomical production, and replace it preferably with cooperative, technical and intensive production, which would bring with it the advantages of large-scale production.
      • Establish measures to prevent the future alienation of Cuban lands into the hands of foreigners.

      Provisions

      The Agrarian Reform Law outlawed large landholdings in Cuba, setting the maximum limit of caballerías (approximately 30 hectares) that could be owned by individuals or legal entities. The lands of those exceeding this limit were nationalized and distributed among peasants and agricultural workers. As stipulated by the Law, lands belonging to the State and municipalities were also subject to redistribution.

      No se consideraron nacionalizables las áreas concedidas en propiedad a cooperativas agrícolas de producción organizadas por el Instituto Nacional de Reforma Agraria, para la explotación de tierras del Estado; las del Estado, Provincias y Municipios que estuvieren dedicadas o se dedicaren a establecimientos públicos o de servicio general a la comunidad; los montes cuando se declarasen incluidos en las reservas forestales de la nación, sujetos para aprovechamiento, utilidad pública o explotación determinados por la Ley y las de comunidades rurales destinadas a satisfacer fines de asistencia social, educación, salud y similares, previa declaración de su carácter por el Instituto Nacional de Reforma Agraria, y sólo en la extensión requerida para esos fines.

      No se consideraron a los efectos de la determinación del límite máximo de treinta caballerías, las áreas necesarias para establecimientos industriales enclavadas en las fincas rústicas, así como para sus bateyes, oficinas y viviendas; así como tampoco las zonas urbanizadas en el interior de las fincas rústicas y las que por acuerdo del Instituto Nacional de Reforma Agraria se destinaron a crear caseríos o núcleos de población rural en las Zonas de Desarrollo Agrario; o donde existían otros recursos naturales susceptibles de ser explotados en previsión del desarrollo futuro del país, a juicio del Instituto Nacional de Reforma Agraria.

      A partir de la promulgación de la Ley quedó prohibido establecer contratos de aparcería u otros en los que se estipulase el pago de la renta de las fincas rústicas en forma de participación proporcional en sus productos.

      Fueron exceptuados del margen máximo de 30 caballerías:

      "Las áreas sembradas de caña, cuyos rendimientos no sean menores del promedio nacional, más de un 50%.

      Las áreas ganaderas que alcancen el mínimo de sustentación de ganado por caballería que fije el Instituto Nacional de Reforma Agraria, atendido el tipo racial, tiempo de desarrollo, por ciento de natalidad, régimen de alimentación, por ciento de rendimiento en gancho en el caso vacuno destinado a carne, o de leche, en el caso de vacuno de esa clase. Se considerarán las posibilidades del área productora de que se trate por medio del análisis físico químico de sus suelos, la humedad de los mismos y régimen de las lluvias.

      Las áreas sembradas de arroz que rindan normalmente no menos del 50% sobre el promedio de producción nacional de la variedad de que se trate, a juicio del Instituto Nacional de Reforma Agraria.

      The areas dedicated to one or more crops or agricultural exploitation, with or without industrial activity, for whose efficient exploitation and rational economic performance it is necessary to maintain an extension of land greater than that established as the maximum limit in Article 1 of this Law." [2]
